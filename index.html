Coral Reefs & Ocean Warming

Understanding how rising sea temperatures threaten Earth's underwater ecosystems

When Oceans Heat, Corals Suffer

Coral reefs depend on a delicate temperature balance. When sea surface temperatures rise just 1-2°C above normal for extended periods, corals expel their symbiotic algae (zooxanthellae) and turn white—a process called coral bleaching. Without these algae, corals lose their primary food source and become vulnerable to disease and death.

Learn the Science

What is Coral Bleaching?

Primary Drivers

  • Elevated Sea Surface Temperatures (SST): The most significant cause, driven by climate change and marine heatwaves
  • Solar Radiation: High light levels combined with heat stress intensify bleaching
  • Ocean Acidification: Reduced pH from absorbed CO₂ weakens coral skeletons

Compounding Factors

  • Pollution and nutrient runoff
  • Overfishing and ecosystem imbalance
  • Physical damage from storms and human activity
  • Disease outbreaks in stressed populations

Consequences

  • Biodiversity Loss: Reefs support 25% of marine species
  • Fisheries Decline: Over 1 billion people depend on reef fish for protein
  • Coastal Vulnerability: Loss of natural storm protection
  • Economic Impact: Tourism and fishing industries affected

Key Threshold: Degree Heating Weeks (DHW)

Scientists measure thermal stress using Degree Heating Weeks—the accumulated heat above the normal maximum temperature over a 12-week period. A DHW of 4°C-weeks typically triggers bleaching; 8°C-weeks or higher often causes mortality.

What You Can Do

Everyone can play a role in protecting coral reefs and combating ocean warming:

  • Reduce Carbon Emissions: Support renewable energy, reduce personal carbon footprint, and advocate for climate policy
  • Support Reef Monitoring & Restoration: Contribute to organizations working on coral research, transplantation, and assisted evolution
  • Practice Reef-Safe Tourism: Use reef-safe sunscreen, avoid touching corals, and choose eco-certified tour operators
  • Reduce Ocean Pollution: Minimize plastic use, properly dispose of chemicals, and support watershed protection
  • Spread Awareness: Share information about reef conservation and ocean warming with your community

Glossary

SST (Sea Surface Temperature)
The temperature of the top layer of the ocean, typically measured at depths of 1-20 meters.
DHW (Degree Heating Weeks)
A measure of accumulated thermal stress, calculated as the number of degrees Celsius above the bleaching threshold, summed over a 12-week period.
ENSO (El Niño-Southern Oscillation)
A climate pattern involving fluctuations in ocean temperatures in the equatorial Pacific, which can trigger widespread coral bleaching during El Niño events.
MHW (Marine Heatwave)
Prolonged periods of anomalously warm ocean temperatures that can last weeks to months, causing severe stress to coral reefs.
Calcification
The process by which corals build their calcium carbonate skeletons. This process is impaired by warming waters and ocean acidification.
Zooxanthellae
Symbiotic algae living within coral tissue that provide up to 90% of the coral's energy through photosynthesis.
